# Voiceover script

Eight short lines, one per scene, about 60 words across the video's 34.8 seconds. That's a relaxed pace with room to
breathe between lines. At a normal speaking pace, about 2.6 words a second, every line finishes inside its scene. The voice doesn't read the headlines on screen word for word; it says what the picture shows.

| # | File | Starts at | Must finish by | Line |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | `vo-1-hook` | 0.2 s | 2.9 s | Hours of lectures. Notes you never reread. |
| 2 | `vo-2-reveal` | 3.2 s | 5.9 s | Meet Study Stash. |
| 3 | `vo-3-record` | 6.3 s | 11.8 s | Hit record. It transcribes, writes the notes, and files them by class. |
| 4 | `vo-4-diagrams` | 12.3 s | 17.8 s | Even the diagrams: labelled anatomy for nursing, graphs and formulas for calculus. |
| 5 | `vo-5-ask` | 18.3 s | 23.2 s | Then ask your notes anything, with Claude or OpenAI. |
| 6 | `vo-6-canvas` | 23.6 s | 27.4 s | Plus Canvas: what's due, and how it's graded. |
| 7 | `vo-7-promise` | 27.8 s | 29.9 s | Your recordings never leave your computers. |
| 8 | `vo-8-cta` | 30.4 s | 34.3 s | Study Stash. Free, for Mac and Windows. |

Line 7 says "your computers" (plural) because it's true in both setups: just a laptop, or a laptop and a library at
home. Transcription happens on the computer, so the audio never goes anywhere else.

## Voice

- Warm, calm and conversational: a student-friendly narrator, not an advert. Think of an Apple product film, a
  little warmer. No hype, no rising "announcer" tone.
- Same voice and the same settings for all eight lines, so they sound like one take.
- A starting point in ElevenLabs:
  - Stability around 50%. Lower is more expressive, higher is steadier.
  - Similarity around 75%.
  - Style low, 10–25%.
  - Speaker boost on.
- Say "Study Stash" clearly, with a slight lift on "Stash" in line 2. "Claude" is said like "clawed"; if it comes out
  wrong, type it as "Clawd".
- Leave a beat of silence at the start and end of each file. Trimming it is easy; adding it back isn't.

## Handing the files back

Export each line as its own file: WAV or MP3, 44.1 or 48 kHz. Name them as in the table, e.g.
`vo-3-record.wav`. Put them in `promo-video/public/audio/vo/`, or send them over.

Then each line is placed on its scene. The music dips under the voice and comes back up between lines, and the sound
effects are eased back a little. If a line runs long, the pause is trimmed or that scene is lengthened by a beat, and
the music follows.
